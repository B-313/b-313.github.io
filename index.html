About Me

I solve problems people actually adopt, speaking business & tech with joy.

I have learned something counterintuitive: systems rarely fail because they're poorly designed. They fail because they don't honor how people actually work.

At Taj Hotels in Mumbai, I learned service operations through a paradox: delivering seamless experiences required understanding that chaos exists within structure. You can't architect perfect service by perfecting systems. You architect it by understanding what guests actually need, then building processes around that. Across hotels and cities, this taught me my first principle: listen to how people work, then design for it. That principle deepened during an unexpected pressure test. In 2017, India's demonetization fractured payment pipelines overnight. At Taj Wellington Mews, I watched sales teams navigate impossible uncertainty. In that chaos, I realized: the organisations that survived weren't those with the most sophisticated systems. They were the ones who had mapped their processes deeply enough to understand where they were fragile. Process thinking became my compass for resilience.

I carried both lessons to Australia for my Master's at Monash University. But I didn't just study business strategy, I immersed myself in collaboration; Leading Partnerships at TEDxMonashUniversity, volunteering with Graduate Association, performing with Monash University Student Theatre Group, as well as taking a Study Abroad module in Europe. I learned that adaptability isn't a skill. It's a mindset that comes from genuine exposure to different ways of thinking.

Then came Infosys Business Process Management. I worked across Telecommunications and Healthcare projects, regulated industries where process gaps don't create frustration; they create compliance risk. I managed stakeholders across offshore and onshore teams. But the moment that crystallised everything: I was looking at spreadsheets of operational data, and I realised these weren't just numbers. Every row was a bottleneck. Every column was a user pain point. The spreadsheet was a map of human friction. That's when I understood: Data is a window into where people are stuck.

Now, in data science and AI, I'm pursuing the logical next step. If spreadsheets revealed human friction, then machine learning and pattern discovery can transform that friction into flow. But not with algorithms alone. With algorithms designed for adoption, transparent enough for people to understand, governed enough for people to trust, built closely enough with teams that they believe in it.

My Approach

    The bridge between business and technical thinking isn't a soft skill for me. It's the centre of everything I do. It's how I diagnose real problems instead of building solutions in isolation. It's how I ensure what I build actually gets used. That's the work I'm drawn to. The moment data tells a story that changes how an organisation operates. The moment a system becomes invisible because it works so well people forget it's there. The moment trust turns skepticism into adoption.
